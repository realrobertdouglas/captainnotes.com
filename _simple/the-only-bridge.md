---
title: "The Only Bridge, for a five-year-old"
---

Imagine there is only one bridge across a river. Whoever owns the bridge can charge whatever they like, up to what it would cost you to swim. That is why people build bridges where there are none: the first bridge earns a lot. It is also why owning the only bridge is a bit like being a king.

Denmark owned a narrow bit of sea that every ship had to pass through to reach the Baltic. For four hundred years, every ship paid a toll. There was a clever rule: you told the toll collector what your cargo was worth, and the king could buy it from you at that price. So you did not lie low, and you did not lie high. In the end, the other countries did not fight for the passage. They paid Denmark one big sum, and the passage was free forever.

Bandwidth Swap pays the only link across a gap the most, because that is what makes people build links. It does not stop that link from charging a lot. What it does is let anyone build a second link, and let anyone promise to pay for a link that does not exist yet, which helps someone build it.

So the paper should say three things plainly. The only bridge is a king until the second bridge comes. The promise to buy on a new route is how you start the second bridge. And paying the bridge once for passage forever, like Denmark, is a thing the paper already allows and should name.
