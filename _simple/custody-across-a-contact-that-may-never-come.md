---
title: "Custody Across a Contact That May Never Come, for a five-year-old"
---

Imagine you give a package to a camel driver who crosses a desert. The package is not his. But for the whole trip he is the one holding it, and you are far away. That is called custody: looking after something that belongs to someone else until you can hand it on.

What if the camel never arrives? Long ago, sailors had an answer. A sailor borrowed money for a voyage. If the ship sank, he did not have to pay it back. If the ship came home, he paid back extra. The extra from the ships that came home paid for the ships that did not. The lender was not selling time. The lender was buying the sea.

Ship captains also signed a paper saying exactly what they had loaded. If the cargo went missing, the paper proved it.

Bandwidth Swap has computers that hold your data until the next link opens, like a camel waiting for the next oasis. The paper says the computer should hold your data because the next step is already paid for. Good. But it does not say what happens if the next link never opens. Who loses? Right now, you do, and nobody told you the price.

Three things would fix it. The computer signs for what it is holding, like the captain. The rules say how long it has to hold it. And the links that work pay a little extra for the links that don't, like the sailors did.
