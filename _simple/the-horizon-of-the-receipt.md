---
title: "The Horizon of the Receipt, for a five-year-old"
---

Bandwidth Swap pays a computer for carrying your data once you sign that the data arrived. The signing takes as long as a message there and back. To the Moon that is seconds. To Mars it is under an hour. To the nearest star it is eight and a half years.

Now imagine a star so far away that the answer comes back after everyone who sent the message is gone. The receipt can still be made, one link at a time. But something important has changed. The whole idea of the paper is that the person who paid is the one who cannot be fooled about whether the data arrived. Past that far-away line, the person who wanted the data could not have paid, because they would have had to pay a lifetime early. So the sender pays, and the receiver gets a present.

That is what happened in 1974, when a big dish on Earth sent a message toward a cluster of stars twenty-five thousand light-years away. People were paid to build the dish. Nobody was paid for delivery. Nobody ever will be. It was a gift.

The paper is right that the receipt stays the same at any distance. But it should draw a line and say: inside this line, the payment proves the delivery. Past it, the payment is just hope. There is nothing wrong with hope. Most things ever sent a long way were sent on it. The paper should just say which side of the line each place is on.
