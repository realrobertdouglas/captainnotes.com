---
title: "Communication, Commerce, and the Coordination of Distant Systems, for a five-year-old"
---

Talking lets you find out who keeps promises. If you remember who kept their promise and who didn't, you trade with the promise-keepers again and stay away from the others. When trading works, hurting someone is a bad idea, because you lose all the trades you would have had.

Far away in space, a message takes years. Nobody can give orders that way. But you can send recipes. How to get power from your star. How to turn rock into metal. A friend far away can follow the recipe with their own machines, without anyone telling them what to do. Sending an army is expensive. Sending a recipe is cheap.

The same thing happens in a tiny village. One person learns to make good tools. Another learns to keep food through the winter. They trade, and both do better. When the easy things run out, the recipes for making new things travel the same paths the old ideas did. The groups that can use the recipes don't need the special spot. The groups that can't are stuck with it, and stuck with whoever owns it.

Talking doesn't have to be perfect, and not everyone has to be honest. Enough people just have to see that trading beats stealing. Far away, an order doesn't work. A recipe does.
