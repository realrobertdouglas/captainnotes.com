---
title: "Anarchy Across the Stars, for a five-year-old"
---

Imagine people live on stars very, very far apart. If you send a letter to a friend on a far star, it takes twenty years to get there. Twenty more years for the answer. By the time a boss on one star says "do this," everyone on the other star has grown up and moved on.

So nobody far away can really be the boss of you. Sending big ships to make people listen costs more than anything the boss would get back. People far away end up running things themselves.

Does that mean everyone fights? Fighting is always possible. But there is something better: trading. If you trade your apples for your neighbor's bread every week, you don't want to hurt your neighbor. Then there would be no more bread, and everyone else would hear that you are mean, and nobody would trade with you.

A few things help trading work. Everyone knows whose stuff is whose. There is a fair way to settle arguments, or you can just walk away. Anyone who wants to can join in. These don't stop all fights, but they make fighting a bad deal.

This is true for aliens, and it is true for the next town over. A big council can still meet for the few things everyone has to decide together. Everything else gets decided at home, by people who would rather trade with their neighbors than boss them around.
