---
title: "Every Delayed Exchange Is a Loan, for a five-year-old"
---

When you buy a cookie, two things happen. You hand over your coin, and you get your cookie. At the shop they happen at the same time, so nobody thinks about the gap.

Now imagine your friend lives on a far star. You send the coin, and the cookie comes back twenty years later. For twenty years your friend has your coin and you have nothing. That is a loan, even if nobody calls it one. Whoever goes first is lending.

Long ago, merchants figured this out. Carrying heavy coins across the sea was dangerous, so they invented a paper note instead. You paid a banker at home, and the banker's friend far away paid your seller a month later. The note carried the money, waited the month, and swapped the coins, all at once. And the wait had a price hidden inside it.

A ship company in Holland went further. Its trips took two years, so it made the company itself last longer than any trip. Then people could wait, because the company would still be there.

Bandwidth Swap pays a computer for carrying your data once you sign that it arrived. Between planets, that signature takes a long time to come back. So somebody is lending. The paper says who waits. It does not say what the waiting costs. A long wait should have a price written down, the way the old paper note had one. That is the thing to add.
