---
title: "Enforcement at a Distance Is Exclusion, for a five-year-old"
---

If someone cheats at a game, a teacher can take away their turn. But what if the cheater lives on a far star, and no teacher can get there? Nobody can take anything away from them.

Long ago, merchants had this problem all the time. Their answer was simple: if you cheat one of us, none of us will ever trade with you again. That was the whole punishment. It worked because everyone wanted to keep trading. Some towns kept a book of who had cheated, so you could check before you made a deal. Being written in the book was the punishment.

Bandwidth Swap has bookkeepers called Mints. Each Mint keeps a deposit from the computers near it, and if one cheats, the Mint takes the deposit. But a Mint on Earth cannot take a deposit from a Mint on Mars. What it can do is tell everyone, and stop talking to the cheater.

Here is the clever part. The computers that carry messages between Earth and Mars have deposits with both Mints. So when the Earth Mint says "that one cheated," those computers leave the cheater fast, or they lose their deposit at home.

The paper calls this "taking the deposit." Really it is shutting the door. The paper should use the right word. And it should be careful: a club that never lets anyone back in is just a new kind of boss.
