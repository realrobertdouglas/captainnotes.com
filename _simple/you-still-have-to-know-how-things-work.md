---
title: "You Still Have to Know How Things Work, for a five-year-old"
---

One day the computer program at our work got stuck, three times. Each time we turned it off and on again, and it worked. But that was not really the fix. Something inside was holding a door shut, and everything else was waiting in line behind it. Turning it off let go of the door. It also wiped away the clues about who was holding it.

Computers that can write, like AI, can now make lots of things for you very fast. So making a thing is the easy part now. The hard part is telling whether the thing is right. You can only tell if you know how the thing works.

If you don't know how it works and it breaks, you just keep asking the computer to try again. That is guessing. It is slow, and you might never get it right.

Some people will use AI to pretend they know how to do things they don't. Their work will look good at first. Then it will break later, and somebody else will have to fix it. That is called slop.

You don't have to know everything. Just know a little more than the job you are doing. And when the computer helps you, ask it why, so you learn.
