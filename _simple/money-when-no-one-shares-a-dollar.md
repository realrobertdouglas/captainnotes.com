---
title: "Money When No One Shares a Dollar, for a five-year-old"
---

On Earth, Bandwidth Swap pays in dollars. But what if the other end of the link is on Mars, and Mars does not use dollars? Then a price in dollars means nothing over there.

People have hit this problem for a very long time. Sometimes they fixed it by counting in a unit that was not a real coin at all, just a number everyone used for adding up. Sometimes they used a coin anyone could weigh, so you did not have to trust where it came from. And sometimes they trusted a bookkeeper whose ledger everyone believed.

Now look at the rules in the paper. Some rules are amounts, like "ten cents a gigabyte." Some rules are fractions, like "five percent of every payment is burned." When you cross to Mars, the amounts stop making sense. The fractions still work, because five percent is five percent in any money. And the rule that pays the smaller of two counts is in bytes, which both sides can count.

So the parts of the paper that are fractions are the real protocol. The parts that are amounts are just what Earth chose.

The computer that carries data between Earth and Mars holds money on both sides. That makes it the money-changer, whether anyone meant it to be or not. The paper should say so, and say which unit a link uses, the way it already says how far the link is.
