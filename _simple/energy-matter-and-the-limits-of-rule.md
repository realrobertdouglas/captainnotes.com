---
title: "Energy, Matter, and the Limits of Rule, for a five-year-old"
---

Energy is what lets you change things. Some places come with easy energy: coal in the ground, rivers that fall, sunshine that plants soak up. Places with easy energy and lots of plants and animals and good dirt get a head start. They can grow, learn, and build before they have to work hard for anything.

But easy things run out. The dirt gets tired. The coal gets dug up. Then you need new energy, or a way to turn the stuff you have into the stuff you need.

With a lot of energy, the kind a star makes, you can turn plain rocks into useful things. Then rocks aren't junk anymore. Asteroids and comets are ingredients. A whole star system is a giant box of building blocks.

A grown-up civilization doesn't need the one special field or the one special mine anymore. It needs machines, and energy to run them, and it makes everything else from whatever is around.

Bosses used to be powerful because they owned the special spot. The good valley. The mine. The oil. If you needed it, you paid them. But when anyone can make what they need out of rocks, the special spot stops being special. The things that are still rare are knowing how, having the machines, and keeping them safe. The rocks are everywhere. The know-how is not.
